# ADR 0022: Static obstacles of several kinds, placed by biome

Status: accepted. Follows [ADR 0019](0019-line-of-sight.md) (line of sight, which promised that boulders would reuse its blocker test with a per-kind `blocksSight`), [ADR 0021](0021-food-patches-and-plants.md) (food in patches and plants, whose measurements named obstacles and predation as the next levers).

## The problem

[ADR 0021](0021-food-patches-and-plants.md) made food worth walking to, and measured that part variety still did not move: part lists went from 21.6 to 26 to 28 inside the noise, and bodies ended with about 11 of 16 part types from every start. Nothing in the world was in the way: sight went straight through everything but other creatures, and a body went straight to what it saw. A long-range eye, an eyestalk, a good turn rate or a strong thrust had nothing to be better at.

## Decision

1. **One shape, several kinds (`ecology.obstacles`).** Every obstacle is a circle `{id, kind, x, y, radius, blocksMovement, blocksSight}` in a spatial index built once at world creation (`ecology/ObstacleField.ts`). A kind is a **recipe** and two **flags**, so a new kind is data and not code: `boulder` (one circle, blocks both), `tree` (one small circle, blocks both), `ridge` (a chain of four to eight overlapping circles of one size: a wall, blocks both), `pond` (one large circle that blocks movement but **not sight**: you see across water) and `reeds` (a tight cluster that blocks sight but **not movement**: cover you can walk through). Each kind has its own radius range, members range and a density for each of the six biomes.
2. **Placement by biome.** Once, at creation, from the `terrain` stream, after the region map and before the static hazards. For each kind in a fixed order the world draws `round(maxDensity x area / 10,000)` candidate sites, six floats each (x, y, acceptance, radius, members, angle), so the draw count depends only on the configuration and the field size and never on what was accepted. A site is accepted with probability `density(biome at the site) / maxDensity`, then rejected without a draw if it would leave the clearance from the edge (20 units), overlap an earlier site, or push the field over `maxCoverage` (4 percent). A region-less world uses the mean of a kind's densities. Counts scale with the area by construction (densities are per 10,000 square units), so `fieldScaling` needs no change. The cluster layout, which has no region map, has no obstacles.
3. **Movement.** After the move and the world clamp, a body is pushed out of every circle that blocks movement to its own radius plus the circle's (a juvenile's smaller radius for a juvenile, as eating already does), along the line from the circle's centre, in id order, three passes so the corner of a wall cannot leave it inside a neighbour. A body slides along an edge and stops head-on. A pushed body reports the velocity it **achieved** (no faster than its top speed), so the brain's velocity inputs and the energy cost agree with what happened. Every circle is at least 6 units in radius (validated), because a body moves at most about 2 units a tick and a one-step push-out cannot be tunnelled through a circle that size. Newborns appear beside a parent after the move and are resolved by their first move.
4. **Sight.** `PerceptionSystem` takes a blockers struct: other creatures (when `perception.organismsBlockSight` is on) and the circles whose kind blocks sight. A circle on the line from the eye to a food item or a peer hides it, whether or not creatures hide each other; the occluding search (nearest first, the first not hidden, at most `maxSightChecks` looked at) is used whenever either blocker exists and not otherwise. A viewer inside a circle is not blinded by it. Hazards, the mating signal and the home sense are not vision and are not hidden.
5. **Food and plants.** A food site, and a plant hub with room for its ring of nodes, is never inside a circle that blocks movement: the noise and kinds paths reject the candidate with the draws they already make; the plain two-draw uniform path drops the spawn.
6. **No new brain input (wall avoidance).** A wall sense would cost a hash-schema bump, 32 more brain weights, a shifted genetics draw count (every founder changes) and every long-run gate re-measured, for a benefit not shown. Bodies feel obstacles through the honest velocity inputs, through sight occlusion (they cannot see food behind a rock, so they route to what they can see) and through the reward. The obstacle probe measures the **pinned** share (creatures held within a body-length of a rock's edge for a whole 20-tick window while barely moving): 0.7 percent at the shipped density. If that grows, the obstacle sense goes into Step 7 with the other senses, so the schema bumps once.
7. **Drawing.** The field is static, so it goes to the renderer once with the world (`World.obstacles()`, `renderer.setWorld`) and is drawn once, in flat colours by kind (rocks, trees and ridges solid; ponds and reeds translucent), on a new `obstacles` layer between hazards and food, with its own toggle.
8. **Presets.** `balanced`, `lush`, `harsh`, `rapid-evolution` and `fixed-bodies` use the default field; `classic` (the Phase 3 world) and `clusters` have none.

The first guess (densities that would cover 7.8 percent of the field with the cap lifted) was too heavy: the lowest population fell to 7 to 10 and the later kinds (ridges, ponds, reeds) were cut off by the cap, which fills in kind order. The shipped densities are a quarter of that (about 53 circles and 2.4 percent coverage on a 700 x 700 field, boulders 0.9, ridges 0.6, reeds 0.3, ponds 0.3, trees 0.25 percent), with the cap at 4 percent as a safety net rather than the arbiter. `harsh` (8 seeds) was measured three ways. With obstacles off: mean population 41.3, lowest 10, 0 extinct. With **twice** the boulders and ridges (the first idea for a harsher field): 34.9, lowest 0, **1 of 8 worlds extinct**, so that override was dropped (an extinct world is a failure, not a number to re-pin). With the shipped default field: 39.0, lowest 8, 0 extinct, starved 54 percent, 0.9 percent pinned; it is still harsher than `balanced` (60.6) through its food and storms alone.

## Findings, stated plainly

- **Part variety did not move in a way the data can separate from chance.** At the shipped density the part columns are nominally up (types 11.8 to 12.4, lists 27.1 to 29.3, idle-spin 15.8 to 13.6 percent, lost-spin 3.8 to 3.0 percent) but 16 seeds cannot separate that from noise, and every heavier setup moved the lists _down_ (20 to 24). So obstacles are not, at this strength, a lever on part variety: what the data supports is that a light field leaves the economy intact and gives sight something to do. If part variety is the goal, predation is the next lever, and it is next in the queue.
- **Heavy fields hurt before they help.** At 3 to 6 percent coverage a world's lowest population fell to 7 to 10 (against 18 to 22 with none): rocks hide food and make detours costly, and a small population has no slack.
- **Pinned creatures are rare** (0.7 percent at the shipped density, 2.2 percent at 6 percent coverage), so the case for an obstacle sense is weak at this density and is left for Step 7 to decide with numbers.
- **A cost.** The default world ran at about 830 ticks a second against 930 with obstacles off (the two ran side by side on a shared machine, so read it as roughly a tenth): a hash query per body per tick in movement and one per candidate in sight.
- **A test that caught a real mistake.** A newborn is placed beside its parent after movement, so for one tick it can overlap a rock; the whole-world overlap test found it, and the design is that its first move resolves it.
- **A deviation from the plan.** The plan said hazards would be placed from the stream after the obstacles' draws had been added; the obstacles are generated first (regions, obstacles, hazards), so with obstacles on the hazards land elsewhere. With obstacles off nothing is drawn and nothing moves.

## Verification

- **Switch-off proofs.** Commit 1 and commit 2 each reproduce all 41 golden entries with obstacles off (the default at the time); a world with obstacles on but every density zero has the same state hash as the world without them. The golden entries are all pinned to `obstacles.enabled: false` when the default flips, and all 39 still reproduce their pinned hashes; five new entries cover the new default.
- **The default flip.** Every golden entry is pinned to `obstacles.enabled: false` and all 39 still reproduce their original hashes (`withoutEcology`, which the Phase 3 and Phase 5 pins use, switches obstacles off too); five new entries (`obstacles-*`) cover the new default. The full `pnpm test:determinism` passes to completion (17 files, 87 tests) with no statistical gate re-pinned. One unit test did move: the exit-criteria claim that the fungus diet belongs to the dark and the hot kept its 0.6 bar but is now made for cave (175 of 239 meals, 0.73) and for the cave-and-volcanic niche pooled (200 of 295, 0.68), because volcanic, a small patch, now has twice the meals and 25 of 56 (0.45) are fungus (it was 16 of 26, 0.62).
- **Movement:** rim, slide, corner of a wall, no tunnelling at the smallest radius and top speed, a body spawned inside, honest velocity, reeds pass, juveniles get closer, order of circles. **Sight:** rock, pond (seen across), reeds (hidden), fallback to the next food, the check budget, peers, a viewer inside a rock, creatures and rocks together. **Placement and worlds:** the generator is a pure function of seed and config; food, hubs and nodes never sit in a rock over 2,000 ticks on three seeds on all three placement paths; bodies are never left inside a rock after their first tick; the free space of 32 seeds stays one connected piece (at least 95 percent); chunked, stepped and one-go runs end in the same hash.

## Consequences

- A body can be held against a rock, and food can sit just behind one. Both are rare at the shipped density and are measured by the probe.
- Moving (kinematic) obstacles, the obstacle sense and obstacles in the cluster layout are not here. Moving ones need hashed state and a per-tick index rebuild.
- Bites ignore obstacles: a creature can bite food just across a thin ridge. Small, and not fixed here.
- A new obstacle kind is a config entry (a recipe and two flags); a new recipe is a function in `ObstacleField.layout`.
